# GOD, SATAN, AND THE DEPARTMENT OF DIVINE FACEBOOK ENGAGEMENT
### Or: How the Creator of the Universe Apparently Became a Social Media Influencer

Well, I'll be damned.

Apparently, God Almighty—the eternal, omnipotent, omniscient creator of approximately two trillion galaxies, give or take a cosmic rounding error—has taken a personal interest in my Facebook feed.

According to this inspirational little theological fortune cookie, God specifically arranged for me to encounter it **TODAY, NOT TOMORROW.**

Extraordinary.

One might reasonably wonder why the architect of spacetime, who allegedly knows every hair on every head, would need to communicate through a recycled Facebook graphic rather than simply addressing the matter directly.

Perhaps the heavenly communications department has suffered budget cuts.

Perhaps Gabriel has retired.

Perhaps the Holy Spirit has been outsourced to Meta.

Whatever the explanation, here we are.

And apparently, Satan is involved.

Naturally.

Because no inspirational religious chain letter is complete without a supernatural villain whose eternal plans can be thwarted by typing **AMEN** beneath a photograph of highlighted Bible verses.

But before we get to His Infernal Majesty and his apparently catastrophic engagement statistics, let's examine what the Bible actually says.

## GOD IS EVERYWHERE. EXCEPT, APPARENTLY, WHERE THE MEME SAYS HE ISN'T.

Genesis begins with God creating the heavens and the earth.

Psalm 139 asks where anyone could flee from God's presence. The answer, rather inconveniently for anyone claiming exclusive divine companionship, is nowhere.

Acts 17:28 declares that in God we live, move, and have our being.

Colossians 1:17 says that all things hold together in Christ.

Now, these passages don't necessarily teach that God *is* every object in existence. Classical Christian theology distinguishes the Creator from creation.

But they certainly present God as universally present and sustaining existence.

Which raises a fascinating question:

**If God is everywhere, how exactly does one arrange to be somewhere God isn't?**

Does God maintain restricted-access areas?

Are there neighborhoods where the Almighty refuses to deliver?

Does the divine presence disappear when somebody votes differently, joins another religion, questions church doctrine, or declines to type AMEN beneath a Facebook post?

The biblical texts don't support such a simplistic geographical arrangement.

More importantly, **God's presence and God's approval are two entirely different propositions.**

A person can believe God sustains their existence without concluding that God endorses their behavior.

Otherwise, every act of cruelty, dishonesty, exploitation, and violence would have to be interpreted as enjoying divine approval simply because its perpetrator remained alive.

And that would be a rather embarrassing theological position.

Especially for a religion that spends considerable time discussing sin.

## LUKE 1:37: A MIRACLE, NOT A BLANK CHECK

Our meme triumphantly announces:

**“For with God nothing shall be impossible.”**

Luke 1:37.

Lovely verse.

But context, that troublesome little bastard, has entered the room.

The passage comes from Gabriel's announcement concerning the extraordinary pregnancies of Elizabeth and Mary.

It's about God's ability to fulfill a divine promise.

It is not a contractual guarantee that every believer will win every argument, escape every consequence, survive every catastrophe, or receive a complimentary parking space outside Walmart.

The meme then offers biblical examples.

Sarah gave birth despite her advanced age.

David defeated Goliath.

Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego survived the furnace.

Lazarus was raised from the dead.

All marvelous stories within their respective religious traditions.

But there's a rather significant problem with using them as proof that divine companionship guarantees worldly protection.

**Job.**

Remember Job?

Righteous fellow. Terrible luck.

Lost his possessions, his children, and his health while the heavenly administration conducted what may be history's least defensible performance review.

Then there are the persecuted prophets.

The martyrs.

The countless faithful people who suffered despite their devotion.

And, of course, there's Jesus.

Who was crucified.

Rather conspicuously.

If divine companionship guaranteed immunity from suffering, the central event of Christianity would be a spectacularly awkward counterexample.

Perhaps the meme's author should have read beyond the inspirational highlights.

## SATAN WOULD LIKE A WORD. ACTUALLY, HE WOULDN'T.

Now we arrive at my favorite part.

**“Disappoint Satan by putting a true Amen!”**

Oh, sweet merciful absurdity.

Apparently, the eternal struggle between good and evil has been reduced to Facebook comment engagement.

Somewhere in the infernal headquarters, a junior demon is nervously approaching the throne.

“Your Darkness, terrible news.”

“What is it, Wormwood?”

“Another seventy-three people typed AMEN beneath a picture of a sunset.”

“NOOOOOOO!”

“I'm afraid so, sir. One of them used six exclamation points.”

“Then all is lost.”

Unfortunately for this theological masterpiece, the history of Satan is considerably more complicated than the meme suggests.

The Hebrew word *satan* originally meant an adversary or opponent.

In portions of the Hebrew Bible, the term describes an adversarial role rather than necessarily identifying the fully developed supernatural enemy familiar from later Christianity.

In Job, *ha-satan*—the accuser—appears among the heavenly beings.

The concept developed across centuries of Jewish and Christian thought.

And then, in 1966, Anton Szandor LaVey founded the Church of Satan.

Here's where things become deliciously interesting.

**The Church of Satan doesn't believe in a literal supernatural Satan.**

Its Satan is symbolic.

He represents individualism, skepticism, pride, liberty, and opposition to certain forms of religious authority.

LaVeyan Satanism is atheistic.

Its adherents do not believe a horned supernatural creature is monitoring Facebook for insufficiently enthusiastic Christians.

In fact, one of LaVey's Nine Satanic Statements declares:

**“Satan represents undefiled wisdom instead of hypocritical self-deceit!”**

Now, the Church of Satan doesn't possess exclusive historical authority over the biblical Satan, and Christian traditions understand the figure very differently.

But it's worth appreciating the irony.

The people who explicitly identify with Satan as a religious symbol generally don't believe in the supernatural being this meme is attempting to irritate.

And the people who do believe in that being apparently think he can be defeated through social-media participation.

What a magnificent species we are.

## GOD KNOWS YOUR HEART. FACEBOOK NEEDS YOUR COMMENT.

Here's another delightful contradiction.

Matthew 6 warns against performing religious devotion merely to be seen by others.

Jesus criticizes ostentatious public piety and emphasizes sincerity over spectacle.

Yet this meme effectively tells people to demonstrate their religious allegiance by making a visible declaration beneath somebody else's content.

Not because they've examined the passage.

Not because they've contemplated its meaning.

Not because they've undertaken an act of kindness, mercy, or justice.

Because they typed four letters.

**A-M-E-N.**

Congratulations.

You've apparently disappointed Satan.

Meanwhile, somewhere in the world, a hungry child remains hungry, a vulnerable person remains unprotected, and someone suffering injustice continues waiting for help.

But at least the algorithm knows you're spiritually committed.

## AND NOW, THE QUESTION NOBODY WANTS TO ANSWER.

If we're going to invoke God as the ultimate moral authority, perhaps we should examine what the scriptures actually demand.

Micah 6:8 speaks of justice, mercy, and humility.

Isaiah 1 condemns religious observance divorced from justice.

Amos 5 rejects worship that coexists with oppression and demands that justice flow like water.

Matthew 25 places extraordinary emphasis on the treatment of hungry people, strangers, prisoners, and those in need.

And 1 John 4:20 delivers a particularly uncomfortable observation:

**Anyone claiming to love God while hating another person is a liar.**

That's rather more demanding than typing AMEN.

It also raises questions about how we evaluate religious and political leaders.

Consider Jeffrey Epstein's documented sexual abuse and trafficking of girls, the criminal conviction of Ghislaine Maxwell, and the institutional failures surrounding those crimes.

Consider the importance of distinguishing established wrongdoing from allegations, social associations, and unsupported accusations.

Consider political leaders, including Donald Trump, whose conduct and exercise of authority have generated extensive litigation, judicial findings, public controversy, and competing claims about legality and morality.

The relevant questions are specific.

What actually happened?

What evidence establishes it?

Who was harmed?

Who bears responsibility?

What laws or moral obligations were violated?

And are we willing to apply those standards consistently, including when the person under scrutiny belongs to our preferred political movement?

**Religious identity is not evidence of innocence. Political allegiance is not a substitute for moral judgment.**

Nor does an expression of faith automatically invalidate someone else's beliefs, experiences, or dignity.

A person can profess profound love for God while behaving cruelly toward other people.

A person can reject supernatural religion while living a life of extraordinary compassion.

And someone can spend every Sunday in church without ever learning the difference between righteousness and self-righteousness.

The Bible itself repeatedly warns about precisely that distinction.

Which brings us to the uncomfortable possibility that the greatest spiritual danger isn't somebody refusing to type AMEN.

It might be somebody who types AMEN enthusiastically while refusing to examine their own conduct.

## THE ALMIGHTY DOESN'T NEED A PUBLIC RELATIONS DEPARTMENT.

If God is omniscient, God already knows what you believe.

If God is omnipresent, God isn't waiting for an algorithm to deliver you into the divine service area.

If God is just, declarations of religious loyalty cannot erase wrongdoing.

And if God is love, perhaps love deserves to be measured by something more substantial than the number of inspirational graphics we circulate.

Love without responsibility is an incomplete moral claim.

Faith without reflection is vulnerable to manipulation.

And professed righteousness without accountability is an invitation to hypocrisy.

None of this requires rejecting God.

It requires taking the moral implications of belief seriously.

So, by all means, believe.

Pray.

Study.

Question.

Love your neighbor.

Feed somebody who's hungry.

Protect somebody who's vulnerable.

Tell the truth when it's inconvenient.

Acknowledge your mistakes.

Make amends when you've caused harm.

And resist the temptation to confuse your own certainty with divine endorsement.

But please, for the love of everything holy, stop pretending the Creator of the Universe requires a Facebook engagement campaign to defeat Satan.

**God, if God exists as described, has considerably more important things to attend to.**

And Satan?

Well, according to the Church of Satan, he doesn't exist as a supernatural being in the first place.

Which means the poor bastard has somehow managed to lose a social-media competition he never entered.

**AMEN.**
